Before Anything Else

The Pipeline

Notion is the single source of truth for all of it, every idea, every script, every editing note, every Frame.io link lives there. Everything else exists to move one piece of content through that same system without anyone having to chase anybody.

A Frame.io link gets dropped into Notion after recording. Zapier sees it and fires off a Slack notification automatically. The editor gets to work. The content strategist reviews it first, I then give final approval, and the content strategist posts.

This is what a Content Ecosystem actually means in practice. It's an efficient base layer even without AI tools like Wideframe or AutoCut layered on top of it. Nothing falls through the cracks because nothing depends on memory.
Two Tools Worth Layering In

Wideframe is an AI coworker for editors, it searches, labels, and organizes raw footage automatically, so an editor isn't burning an hour just hunting for the right clip before they can even start cutting. AutoCut plugs directly into Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve, it strips out dead air and silences on its own, and it can match B-roll to a cut using AI. Neither replaces the pipeline above, they just make the editor's half of it faster.

Tech Stack Notes

The Big Idea

Four tools that keep the ecosystem running while our custom internal SaaS is being built. Each one has exactly one job. The second any of them starts doing someone else's job, the whole system gets confusing fast.

The Rules
Notion
The single source of truth. Every idea, every script, every editing note, every publishing detail lives here and nowhere else.
Frame.io
Where the editor and the content strategist actually review and revise the footage itself.
Zapier
The automation. The second a Frame.io link lands in Notion, it fires off a Slack notification on its own.
Slack
Where the team actually talks, and where everyone gets notified the moment something needs them.

Going Deeper
Communication
I didn't properly convey the idea in the creation, storyboard, and scripting, or I didn't properly give the editing team my exact creative vision, so they failed to execute it.

Section 04

Data & Feedback Notes

The Big Idea

Data is the last step of Execution and the first step of the next Ideation cycle. It closes the loop.

The Rules
Value vs. Virality vs. Conversion, aka The VVC Framework

Three numbers, not one. Views are Virality, how far it traveled, good for reach but it says nothing on its own about whether the video actually did anything. Saves are Value, proof someone found it useful enough to want it again. DMs are Conversion, proof someone wants more than just the video itself. Each number points back to a different reason a video gets made in the first place. A virality piece that racked up views but no saves still did exactly its job. A conversion piece that pulled in DMs off barely any views still won. Match the goal to the number before you judge the performance, then build more of whichever piece actually mattered.

Going Deeper

Feed that back into ideation. Which Pillar performed. Which Packaging location. Which hook type. The data tells you exactly what to build more of.

This is the piece that makes a Content Ecosystem able to run on its own. It learns. It improves. The output gets better every week because the input gets smarter.
