CARD 01 The Storyline Map

This is the foundation of every video. The three opening pieces earn the click, and everything after them is the storyline — Context into Conflict into Clarity into Solution, with each Solution setting up the next Conflict. Short form runs one to three of these cycles. Long form runs multiple, climbing the same shape over and over toward a bigger payoff.

CARD 02 Why It Works — Hook / Retain / Reward

The storyline map lives inside the Hook / Retain / Reward method. The opening system gets the viewer engaged. The Context → Conflict → Clarity → Solution loop keeps them engaged — that entire loop lives inside Retain. And the Clarity beats are the Reward moments: the payoff lines that make the watch feel worth it and earn the next cycle.

CARD 03 The Opening System — Initial Hook / Expansion / Mega Hook

Three hooks, stacked, each one buying the next few seconds. The Initial Hook stops the scroll, the Expansion gives them a reason to believe, and the Mega Hook gives them a reason to stay.

Initial Hook stops the scroll

The very first line. It runs in one of three modes:

Open Loop
Creates an open loop in the viewer's mind that they now want closed. "Should you do xyz?"
Statement
A bold statement that somebody now wants to see play out.
Summary Hook
Summarizes the context of what will be covered.
Expansion gives a reason to believe

Expands on the hook — more context, more clarity, and the credibility that makes the hook land:

"I spent xyz time to learn this."

"It's what helped me gain 500k followers."

Pull from four sources — facts, proof, quotes, or stories:

Fact
High authority.
Psychologists call it dialectical thinking — one of the most advanced ways a human mind can reason.
Proof
Commission screenshot, receipts, real numbers.
It's how Jake added 100k in 4 weeks, Alex added 34K in 10 days, and Rebekah added 10k with 1 post.
Quote
Relatable influence.
Sarah Blakely has this beautiful quote: the more that we experience, the more that we have to offer.
Story
Story of someone of influence.
I just watched the Odyssey and there's one scene I can't stop thinking about.
Mega Hook gives a reason to stay

A third hook that lands after the expansion and pulls them in dramatically. It adds more context and more clarity — but its real job is giving the viewer a deeper reason to stay:

"In a previous video, I broke down my storytelling wheel and said gurus would steal it and sell it. They did."

"So please don't share xyz."

The Pull — four ways to pull them in:

Loss
What it cost you to learn.
I paid 50k just to learn this lesson.
Morals
Transfer responsibility to the viewer.
I'm trusting you with this, please don't abuse it.
Power
What it can do once they have it.
This forces people into a trance.
Secret
Why they've never heard it before.
My 5 year NDA just expired so I can finally share this.
CARD 04 The Retain System — Context / Conflict / Clarity / Solution

This is what bridges the gap after the opening system — the connective tissue of the actual video, and the part that holds the viewer's attention. In Hook / Retain / Reward terms, this whole loop is Retain: Context sets the stage, Conflict creates the tension that keeps them watching, Clarity pays it off (the Reward beat), and Solution resolves it — then hands off into the next Conflict, and the loop runs again.

01 Context signal → a number, a claim, or a question

Cold open. No warm-up, no "hey guys." Land on a real number, a flat declarative claim, or a question aimed straight at the viewer.

Example phrasing

  • ...how I made $12 million at 25
  • Why do you like some creators but not others?
  • Show me another creator that teaches this for free
  • One of the many hills I'm prepared to die on is...
  • There's a kind of genius no IQ test could ever measure...
  • I just watched the Odyssey and there's one scene I can't stop thinking about.
  • I don't see anyone talking about this...
  • It took me 7 months to learn 3 lines that forces people into a trance.
02 Conflict signal → the contrast turn

The pivot into tension. "But" is the most consistent word in the entire system — it's what turns a list of facts into a story. No hard contrast turn usually means no Conflict beat. (Full word bank in Card 05.)

Example phrasing

  • But I held back the most important part.
  • But here's the step that I never showed.
  • But that's not the part I'm talking about.
  • But your competitors are doing the same thing.
  • But most people only say the first.
  • But if you have a trusted brand and an audience that loves you...
  • But it's also why a character in a movie feels real.
  • But the second we make our own content, we forget it.
  • Yet the builder documents their own journey.
  • ...but no one can tell you what it is.
03 Clarity signal → reframe / absolute statement

The one takeaway, landed short — the reframe ("that's not X, it's Y") or the absolute statement (always / never). This is the Reward beat: the payoff line that makes the watch feel worth it, and the beat that quietly sets up the next Conflict.

Example phrasing

  • That's not your taste forming, it's the algorithms.
  • It's that the thing that makes me successful is the same thing that makes me hard to love.
  • A good name a handful of people trust will always beat a famous one millions only clap for.
  • That contradiction is human and it's what makes people trust you.
  • If your life is boring, it's your fault, and it's your responsibility to change it.
  • Removing all the bad consistently by definition makes your content good.
04 Solution signal → the consequence turn

The resolution. "So" signals "here's what I actually did about it" — the mechanism, delivered. Then the Solution hands off: it becomes the setup for the New Conflict, and the loop runs again. (Full word bank in Card 05.)

Example phrasing

  • So to beat them you have to invert.
  • So there are four types of creators.
  • So there's an easy way to have both creator languages...
  • So the real story isn't even about the phone at all.
  • So over the last few years, I have tried to make myself more interesting.
  • So if your life is boring, it's your fault...
  • So when I'm structuring a story or a script, I ask myself...
  • So taste is a skill that can be learned...
  • So your favorite creators are usually storytellers...
CARD 05 The Connective Tissue — Verbiage Bank

The connective tissue is the words that link one beat to the next. It runs on three families — contrast, consequence, and reason — and the rule is simple: connect with these, never with "and then." The anchors (But / So / Because) are the defaults, but rotate through the alternates so scripts never sound templated.

Contrast
enters the Conflict
  • But
  • Yet...
  • However...
  • Except...
  • The problem is...
  • Here's the catch...
  • Turns out...
  • The truth is...
  • What no one tells you is...
  • Until...
  • Instead...
Consequence
delivers the Solution
  • So
  • Which means...
  • That's why...
  • Now...
  • From there...
  • Here's what I did...
  • The fix is...
  • Therefore...
  • Once you know that...
  • That's when...
Reason
justifies the beat
  • Because
  • The reason is...
  • It works because...
  • Since...
  • And that's exactly why...
  • Which is why...

Secret reveal + framework teach "$12M tastemaker"

This secret framework made me a disgustingly good tastemaker and it's how I made $12 million at 25. In a previous video, I broke down my storytelling wheel and said gurus would steal it and sell it. They did.

But I held back the most important part. And I desperately want to see you win, so I'm doing this for you. Everyone says taste is the last thing AI can't replace, but no one can tell you what it is. Taste isn't about how much you've seen because the machine has seen everything. Instead, it's connecting two things that nobody else would.

Here's the part you already know. Every amazing story lives in four parts of your life. It's easy to remember because it literally spells life. Love, identity, fitness, and earnings. You pick a moment in any one of them and go deep by asking yourself why three times to go from the safe answer to the real one to the raw one.

But here's the step that I never showed. Once you have that raw truth, you ask yourself one more thing. Where else in my life does this same truth live? You find it in a completely different area than when you connect the two. That's taste.

Say the moment is I check my phone the second I wake up. The safe reason is because I want to stay on top of things. Well, why? Because if I'm not responsive, I feel like I'm failing people. Really, why? Because somewhere I learned my worth is how useful I am to others. Now, ask yourself, where else does that live? For me, it's love. It's why I overgive, why it can't be loved without earning it.

So the real story isn't even about the phone at all. It's that the thing that makes me successful is the same thing that makes me hard to love. And only I could tell that because no one else has that exact same collision. The storytelling wheel becomes your fingerprint.

So taste is a skill that can be learned by going all the way down in your life and connecting two areas that no one else would ever think to connect.

Four-quadrant taxonomy "creator languages"

Why do you like some creators but not others? This is because the best creators use a primary and secondary creator language, but most creators only use one.

So there are four types of creators. The analyst tells other people's story, but you don't know anything personal about them, so you don't relate or care. Yet the builder documents their own journey. You know a lot about them, but most don't have results, so you're not going to keep watching. The teacher, however, focuses on net new value, but similar to the analyst, it's hard to relate.

So your favorite creators are usually storytellers, where they teach through their own story. You love them because you get net new value and you feel like you actually know them.

So there's an easy way to have both creator languages without making new content. Focus on providing net new value with all your views, but then post one to three stories a day so people can relate.

Compounding list"10 habits for taste"

Script — connective tissue in gold

10 out of 10 habits to build disgustingly good taste, but they become increasingly more niche.

Steal from a world you got no business in. A sport you hate, a genre you never touch.

Because the ideas no one's seen come from connecting two worlds no one thought to combine.

Stop building a mood board of everyone else's life. Keep an archive of your own.

The screenshots, the voice notes, the things only you notice, and that becomes your fingerprint.

Take a song or painting you love and dig into why the artist made it, not just what they made.

Because taste was never copying what was good, it's understanding the why beneath it.

Study the design of the things you touch every day. The cup, the app, the chair.

Someone chose all of it, and once you start seeing the choices, you actually start seeing it appear everywhere.

List everything you love, even the stuff that feels random, and find the thread running through all of it.

Force yourself to get bored. We fill every second, the shower, the walk, the commute with someone else's voice.

And that's not your taste forming, it's the algorithms.

Framework

  1. No hook line — open directly on item 1
  2. Numbered instructions, each with a one-line reason
  3. Escalate specificity — broad/active items first, inward/specific items last
  4. Concrete anchors instead of abstraction
  5. No announced close — final item doubles as thesis

Why it works — Context / Conflict / Solution / Clarity

  • Context — opens on the problem itself: taste gets "increasingly more niche," so it's genuinely hard to build.
  • Conflict — names the default trap without saying it outright: moodboarding "everyone else's life" instead of building your own archive.
  • Solution — the habits, escalating from external (steal from unrelated worlds) to internal (study your own archive, notice design, get bored on purpose).
  • Clarity — final line doubles as thesis: "that's not your taste forming, it's the algorithms" — no announced close needed.
Named concept + reframe"dialectical thinking"

Script — connective tissue in gold

There's a kind of genius no IQ test could ever measure, but it always shows up as a walking contradiction.

The priest who still doubts.

The surgeon who believes in miracles.

The champion who's terrified of losing.

Psychologists call it dialectical thinking.

The ability to hold two opposite truths at once.

It's one of the most advanced ways a human mind can reason because it fights our instinct to resolve contradiction.

But it's also why a character in a movie feels real.

The ones you remember are the ones you can't decide how to feel about. Proud and grieving. Grateful and desperate.

The contradiction isn't a flaw in them. It's the most human thing about them.

But the second we make our own content, we forget it. We decide we have to be one thing.

The expert who already has it figured out. So we hide the other half.

The part that's still unsure and still a little scared.

And that's exactly what makes your content forgettable.

You don't lose your credibility being someone who knows their stuff and is still figuring it out.

That contradiction is human and it's what makes people trust you.

Storytelling is just learning how to show up as a walking contradiction.

Framework

  1. Unexplained-claim hook
  2. Parallel examples — 2-3 quick instances of the same pattern
  3. Name the concept; one-line definition
  4. Bridge the concept to the viewer's domain
  5. Name the common mistake; reframe it as the asset
  6. Maxim close

Why it works — Context / Conflict / Solution / Clarity

  • Context — names real contradictory figures (the doubting priest, the surgeon who believes in miracles) to prove the concept before defining it.
  • Conflict — "the second we make our own content, we forget it" — we hide our own contradiction and perform the finished expert instead.
  • Solution — let the unsure half show: stop deciding you have to be "one thing."
  • Clarity — "that contradiction is human and it's what makes people trust you" — names the exact payoff of the vulnerability.
Compare-contrast micro lesson"Sally & the alligator"

Script — connective tissue in gold

People think you need an entire book to tell a great story, but great storytellers are able to tell a story in a single sentence.

Matter of fact, they do it every sentence.

If you zoomed into an entire story arc, you're going to see many story arcs.

They're supposed to keep you hooked in so you can experience the entire movie, video, or book. Every sentence is a choice, and it depends on what the moment calls for.

Let's take a look at two examples. Exhibit A, Sally followed a squirrel into the mouth of an alligator.

Exhibit B, Sally met the mouth of an alligator because she followed a squirrel.

Each example has their own different functions.

A hooks your attention more, but the reveal is less satisfying.

B doesn't grab attention, but it's really good at pulling you along a thread into a satisfying reveal.

So when I'm structuring a story or a script, I ask myself, what do I need right now?

Do I need the viewer's attention or do I need to keep it going?

Short form content is just the intentional selection of these mini stories to keep your viewer long enough so they can get to the point of most value, whether that's educational or emotional.

Framework

  1. Contrarian claim hook
  2. Zoom metaphor justifying the claim
  3. Two labeled minimal-pair examples — same content, different construction
  4. State the differential function of each version
  5. Generalize into a decision rule; one-line definition close

Why it works — Context / Conflict / Solution / Clarity

  • Context — claims an entire story arc can live inside one sentence, raising a question the viewer wants resolved.
  • Conflict — Exhibit A vs. Exhibit B: the same facts, reordered, produce opposite effects (attention vs. reveal) — proof by direct comparison.
  • Solution — ask "what do I need right now, attention or retention" and choose sentence structure accordingly.
  • Clarity — short-form is reframed as "the intentional selection of mini stories" to carry the viewer to the point of value.

Framework

  1. Hook: contrarian promise; Expansion: friction line

  4. Unique mechanism, often an inversion; contrast; example

  • Clarity — the Munger quote reframes "not stupid" as a bigger edge than "very intelligent," closing on a borrowed-authority thesis.

Script — connective tissue in gold

One of the many hills I'm prepared to die on is that strength training and exercise is not a hobby.

It's not stamp collecting or sewing. It is something everybody should do and it is a part of your hygiene routine.

Just like brushing your teeth or showering. When people treat it as such, I don't even believe that it should be an option.

I believe training your body should be a non-negotiable.

And not in a crazy way, just going to the gym and kind of pushing yourself two or three times a week and walking.

Those two things alone paired with a halfway decent diet that you're conscious of would solve 80% of your problems.

And it's weird when people say like, you're into working out. Fucking yeah, you're not.

Are you not into the fact that your likelihood of surviving all cancers across the board goes up by 20% if you just have a good amount of muscle or that it reduced your risk of all cause mortality, meaning heart attack, stroke, cancer, diabetes, all of it, or the reduced risk of osteoporosis in women, or that you just look fucking sick?

Framework

  1. Declarative "hill to die on" hook
  2. Category reframe — move the subject from optional to mandatory
  3. Concrete minimum-effective-dose
  4. Rhetorical pushback, unfiltered tone
  5. Stacked stats as rhetorical questions
  6. Affectionate callback insult; closing line

Why it works — Context / Conflict / Solution / Clarity

  • Context — declares a "hill to die on": training isn't optional, it's hygiene, non-negotiable.
