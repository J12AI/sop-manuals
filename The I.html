Ideation SOP

Joshua Mendoza Ideation, Creation & Execution Framework

This is the exact ideation system behind every video I make, broken down into simple sections. Everything else, creation and execution, happens after this stage.

Section 01

Where I Get My Content Ideas From

01Onboarding Forms
Every client fills one out before we ever get on a call. Most people think they're hiring you to fix everything, when really they're usually trying to solve one or two specific problems. Read enough onboarding forms back to back and you start noticing the same one or two issues showing up over and over. That's not a coincidence. That's the thing you're actually good at, whether you've named it yet or not. Whatever keeps repeating is your next piece of content.
02Pain Points & Data
The more times you see a specific pain point your ICP keeps running into, the more you need to make content about it specifically. Once is interesting. Three times is a pattern. By the fifth time, it's basically a content calendar handing itself to you. Combine that one pain point with your Pillars and Packaging, and you've got infinite ideas, not just one post.
03Common Questions & Conversations
This one's just about being attentive. On a client call, in a conversation with a friend, catching up with someone in your circle, pay attention to the questions people keep asking you, and the problems or misconceptions you clearly understand that keep resurfacing. The second something shows up more than once, that's not one idea. That's several.
04New Realizations
My best ideas usually come from one of two places, solving a real problem with a client in real time, or sitting inside a mentorship and having something click that I hadn't connected before. Both are proof you're actually growing, not just repeating the same five talking points. Content built from a fresh realization shows your audience exactly where you're at right now, and that's worth more than something you figured out two years ago.
05Deep Thought
Some of my best thinking happens on walks at night, no music, no phone, just me and whatever's around. There's something about fully detaching from screens and cameras that lets my brain actually work through a problem instead of just reacting to it. That's usually where I land on a solution I couldn't see earlier, or start looking at something completely differently than I had an hour before.
On Organic Thoughts

Notice something about all five of these. None of them start with AI generating the concept. That's intentional. I don't want my content built on what a model thinks is interesting. I use Claude to expand and organize ideas that are already mine, pulled from data I'm already collecting: onboarding forms, real conversations, real patterns, real realizations. The idea always comes from me first. This is what I call Organic Thoughts: nothing borrowed, nothing prompted, nothing that didn't already exist in my life before I sat down to write it.

Section 02

The Four Pillars (The What)

A Pillar is the editing style and pacing of the piece, not the topic, not the story. Every concept you'll ever have can be filmed four different ways.

PillarStyleLengthCreated / Documented
Film / CinematicFully planned, storyboarded, cinematicUnder 60sCreated, sometimes Documented
RawHandheld, talking head, organicFlexibleCreated & Documented
1-1 Walkthrough1-2 angles, educational, value-denseFlexibleCreated, sometimes Documented
Hyper-EditedAudio-first or concept-first, fast-pacedFlexibleCreated

Raw lives most naturally in both categories. Film/Cinematic and 1-1 Walkthrough are usually Created, but both can be Documented too, as long as the setup is intentional enough to catch a planned shot in an unplanned moment.

The Pillar doesn't limit your ideas. It multiplies them.
Section 03

The Eight Packaging Types (The How)

Before you say a word, the audience has already decided whether they want to watch you.

That's why Packaging carries just as much weight as the Pillar itself.

One Exception

As a rule, most Pillars work with every Packaging type. The one exception: 1-1 Walkthrough doesn't pair naturally with Walking & Talking or In the Gym. If you're breaking something down on an iPad, a stationary setting will always serve the content better than a moving or high-energy one.

PackagingWhat It Is
Walking & TalkingMoving while speaking, active, energy-forward
The WavePremium architectural backdrop, cinematic angles
Off the CuffPick up the phone, zero planning, bullet points only
PodcastDialogue-style, conversational authority
In the GymHigh-performance, discipline-driven lens
WalkthroughSitting with an iPad or paper, step-by-step delivery
On ZoomRepurposed call footage or an intentional lesson on Zoom
Desk / OfficeClean, professional, approachable authority setup
One concept becomes four different ways to tell it. Each of those four ways can be filmed across eight different locations. From there, your Shot List makes it even more creative. This is how a single idea stops feeling finite.
Section 04

Created vs. Documented

Created

Intentional. You plan it, script it, and go out and make it happen.

Documented

Everything else. You're at the gym, on a Zoom call, talking with a client, sitting in a mastermind, and you're simply recording whatever has substance.

Most people only think in Created. That's the slow way. The biggest influencers combine both. They're filming themselves continuously throughout the day, not just when it's scheduled.
Section 05

The Shot List

If Packaging is the location and Pillar is the format, the Shot List is where you get even more creative when it's actually time to film. Different shots tell different stories. They communicate different emotions subconsciously, before the viewer even processes what you're saying. When you build your own shots based on what you think would actually look good inside a given Packaging type, you're not copying anybody. You're creating something new.

Low Angle Over the Shoulder Point of View Wide Establishing Close-Up Reaction
Essentially, it's the Packaging types on steroids. Same eight locations, same four Pillars, but now every combination has dozens of ways it could actually be framed.
